Drawer 3 — The canvass

Three things gone. Two came back.

Canvass · forty-one pawnshops · six fences · one harbor

The crew took pearls and bearer bonds along with the stone — cover, and cheap cover at that. The pearls surfaced in a Tacoma pawnshop inside a week. Ash from the bonds turned up in a boiler on Dock 6. The men who carried them were paid to be exactly that careless.

The diamond went nowhere. No fence, no cutter, no whisper on any coast. A thirty-four carat stone does not go quiet on its own — it goes quiet when nobody is trying to sell it. The canvass card that proved it is still in the tray underneath, jammed behind the guides.

Drawer 4 — The stone itself

Nobody steals what was never there

Appraisal records · two years back · a quiet sale

I stopped asking where the diamond went and asked where it had been. The appraisal on file with us is two years stale, and the man who signed it left the trade the same month — comfortable, suddenly, in a house no appraiser's salary buys.

The Pembroke Diamond was sold abroad two years ago, quietly, cut into three stones nobody will ever trace. What sat in the Ashford's case — what this city watched get stolen, what we are asked to pay the full sum for — was glass.

Drawer 5 — The name

The man who stole his own stone

Closing note · unsigned · never filed

Pembroke sold the real diamond two years ago to cover what the market took from him. Then he insured the glass, hired a crew to steal it in front of the whole city, and waited for us to pay him the full sum for a stone he had already been paid for once. The pearls, the bonds, the torn page in this file — all of it bought to keep one stale signature from being read twice.

I sat across his desk and said the name out loud. He wrote a number on a card and slid it over. I left the card, mailed back the retainer, and filed the truth where the truth keeps: in a locked drawer, in a dark office, for whoever bothers to read a file all the way through. — T.K.
